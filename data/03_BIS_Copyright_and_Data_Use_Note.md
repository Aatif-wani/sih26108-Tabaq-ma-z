**BIS Data Collection & Copyright/Usage Note — SIH 2026**

*Prepared by: Ilha & Basit (Data Collection + BIS Research) · Problem ID
SIH26108 · For internal team use, not a legal opinion.*

This note is NOT a legal opinion. It exists so the whole SIH 2026 team —
especially Babar, Muhaimin and Dayan — knows what appears safe to use
for the prototype and what genuinely requires BIS's permission before we
build on it. Where a question isn't clearly answered by an official BIS
source, we say so plainly instead of guessing.

1\. The core rule

Copyright in every Indian Standard, and in any other BIS publication,
vests in the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) — not in the public, not
in us, and not in whoever hosts a copy online.

Extracts from Indian Standards can only be reproduced with BIS's due
permission, requested through BIS's own reproduction-request process.

2\. What we can reasonably use as metadata/references

- The standard number (e.g. “IS 456:2000”) — a factual identifier, not
  creative content.

- The official title of the standard, as published.

- Our own short, independently-written summary/category label describing
  what a standard covers (paraphrased in our own words, not copied from
  the standard's scope clause).

- Public metadata already surfaced on BIS's own website: technical
  committee, revision status, certification status
  (mandatory/voluntary), and similar catalogue facts.

- Links to the official BIS page for each standard (Know Your Standard,
  product manuals, circulars).

3\. What may require caution / BIS permission

- Reproducing the full text of any Indian Standard (in the app, in our
  dataset, or anywhere else).

- Copying substantial or verbatim portions of a standard's scope,
  clauses, tables or requirements — even a few sentences quoted directly
  is a reproduction, not a summary.

- Redistributing BIS PDFs (including ones we found hosted on third-party
  or archival sites — the source hosting them does not change BIS's
  copyright).

- Any commercial or public redistribution of standard content beyond
  metadata and links.

- Third-party copyrighted material we might encounter while researching
  (blog summaries, consultancy write-ups, etc.) — we may read these to
  find facts, but we should not copy their wording into our dataset
  either.

4\. What our prototype should do

- Store metadata needed for search (title, our own paraphrase, category,
  keywords) — not full standard text.

- Store and always show links to the official BIS source for every
  result.

- Avoid redistributing full copyrighted standards or large verbatim
  sections in the UI.

- Avoid copying large sections of a standard's clauses into our own
  pages or database.

- Always direct users to the official BIS source for the authoritative,
  complete text.

- Clearly state in the UI that the tool is a recommendation/search aid,
  not legal or compliance approval — a similarity match to a standard is
  not confirmation of compliance with it.

6\. Questions requiring confirmation from BIS

- Whether short, independently-paraphrased scope summaries (a sentence
  or two, not copied wording) fall inside “extracts” requiring
  permission, or are treated as an independent description — the BIS
  copyright page does not draw this line explicitly.

- Whether displaying the standard's official title in a live
  search-result UI (as opposed to a static document) needs separate
  permission, given the title itself is short and largely factual.

- Whether an academic/hackathon prototype (non-commercial, not
  distributed for profit) is treated any differently by BIS than a
  commercial product — we found no BIS statement carving out an
  academic-use exception, so we are not assuming one exists.

*Until these are confirmed, the team's default is the conservative
reading in Sections 2–4 above: metadata and links only, no reproduction
of standard text.*
